## 2. What Alpha is

Alpha is a student-led finance society at Tecnologico de Monterrey, Campus Ciudad de Mexico. It serves primarily the LAF/Finance student community while also making finance more accessible to the wider campus community.

The formal 2026 commitment documents also identify the management project with the **Beca al Talento Estudiantil de Liderazgo**. Treat that as institutional context, not as permission to publish scholarship details about individual members.

Alpha's durable purpose is to strengthen the development of future finance professionals through an ecosystem of:

- professional and career opportunities;
- practical learning outside the formal curriculum;
- relationships with students, alumni, professors, employers, and the financial industry;
- accessible financial education;
- a strong, inclusive, participatory student community.

The management plan groups the society's work into three strategic pillars:

1. **Professional development:** technical workshops, academic support, certifications, interview preparation, CV support, and applied tools such as Excel, Power BI, Python, and financial modeling.
2. **Experience and industry connection:** company visits, talks, networking, alumni engagement, professional opportunities, and exposure to real finance careers.
3. **Inclusion and community:** approachable financial education, content, events, social-impact initiatives, and activities that help students feel connected to the finance community.

When proposing work, connect it to at least one of these pillars and explain the practical benefit for students. Do not create activity merely for visibility or volume.

## 6. How Alpha plans events

Use the existing Drive event documents as the default planning pattern. A useful event brief normally contains:

1. Event title and current status.
2. Date, time, venue, capacity, audience, cost, dress code, and principal owner.
3. Purpose and student value.
4. Detailed run of show with times, activities, owners, and notes.
5. Venue layout, equipment, food, registration, accessibility, and staffing requirements where relevant.
6. A cross-functional checklist with owners and deadlines.
7. Budget, pricing, sponsorships, payments, and reconciliation requirements.
8. Speakers, alumni, partner groups, and approval status.
9. Communication deliverables and publication dates.
10. Open questions, blockers, dependencies, and next decisions.
11. Post-event evidence, financial close, results, feedback, and lessons learned.

When creating or revising a plan:

- Use one clear **directly responsible individual (DRI)** for the event and one owner per action.
- Use exact deadlines instead of `soon` or `as soon as possible` when a date can be set.
- Keep `confirmed`, `tentative`, `requested`, `invited`, and `proposed` visibly distinct.
- Separate decisions from ideas. Fun ideas are welcome, but they remain proposals until feasibility, safety, cost, venue, and institutional constraints are checked.
- Include event-day contingencies for venue, A/V, capacity, connectivity, materials, food, speaker cancellation, and no-shows when relevant.
- Check room capacity, power availability, equipment, access, and setup needs before advertising a final venue.
- Do not publish registration, price, speaker lineup, or location until the responsible owner has verified it.
- After the event, close the loop with attendance, evidence, expenses, outcomes, feedback, and follow-up actions.

## 8. Governance and institutional guardrails

Alpha is autonomous in student leadership but operates inside Tecnologico de Monterrey's institutional processes.

Agents must:

- Preserve professionalism, collaboration, inclusion, respect, plural dialogue, and institutional values.
- Keep Grupos Estudiantiles informed where the formal process requires it.
- Treat the current plan of work, institutional training, reports, approvals, and account controls as real obligations.
- Avoid representing a draft as an official Tec decision or endorsement.
- Avoid signing, accepting commitments, inviting speakers, booking spaces, changing permissions, publishing announcements, or sending external messages unless the user explicitly asks and the required approval is clear.
- Never fabricate a signature, approval, quote, testimonial, attendance number, sponsor, or institutional relationship.
- Preserve continuity: document decisions, owners, artifacts, results, and lessons so the next management team can understand what happened.

The management term is treated as a one-year commitment. Formal documents call for semester reports and continuous feedback. If a member is not meeting responsibilities, the institutional document describes a plan-of-improvement process before continuity in the role is evaluated. Agents may help document facts and draft a plan, but must not make personnel decisions.

## 9. Brand system

The local `Comunicación/Identidad de Marca.pdf` is the visual authority unless a newer approved brand file exists.

### Brand meaning and voice

The Alpha name and symbol reference financial alpha: performance beyond the market, leadership, and the first Greek letter. The voice should feel:

- analytical but disruptive;
- institutional and bold;
- proactive and innovative;
- precise, confident, and contemporary without unnecessary jargon.

Avoid generic corporate language, exaggerated financial claims, empty trend language, or a tone that sounds like an investment guru.

### Visual baseline

- **Classic blue:** `#194270`
- **Black:** `#000000`
- **White:** `#FFFFFF`
- **Light sky blue:** `#AFCFFF`
- **Bright blue:** `#0066FF`
- **Titles:** Poppins, bold, uppercase
- **Body copy:** Montserrat, with selective bold emphasis
- **Alpha logo:** Kollektif

The brand guide includes white, black, classic-blue, sky-blue-fade, and dark-blue-fade applications. The dark-blue-fade treatment is reserved for **Mastering Money**. Preserve the logo's clear space using the guide's `a`-sized spacing rule. Do not redraw, stretch, recolor, or approximate the logo when an approved asset is available.

Example graphics in the brand deck are inspiration, not exact templates. Use depth, shadows, contrast, and blue-toned elements consistently, but do not mechanically copy the examples.

### Publication quality

- Verify all event facts immediately before publication.
- Use the required Tec logos and institutional format.
- Keep public Spanish clear, natural, and correct.
- Ensure dates, times, rooms, costs, registration links, and speaker names agree across every channel.
- Avoid publishing internal comments, unconfirmed details, private contact information, or draft budgets.
- For finance education, explain mechanisms and risks clearly and avoid personalized investment advice or promises of returns.

## 11. Research and factuality

Rules:

- Preserve `TBD` or ask for a decision when a material fact is unresolved.

- Include article/document title, publisher, link, and date when practical.
- Translate financial numbers into student-relevant meaning, but do not distort them.
- Distinguish analysis, inference, proposal, and confirmed fact.
- Treat old scripts and presentation notes as potentially stale and occasionally inaccurate.

## 13. File and document working conventions

### Before starting

- Inspect this file and any nested `AGENTS.md`.
- Inventory the relevant local folder.
- For logistics and team work, inspect the current Drive folder.
- Identify the authoritative artifact and its last-updated context.
- Clarify whether the requested output is a draft, internal working document, approval-ready version, or public deliverable.

### While working

- Preserve existing files and unrelated edits.
- Prefer updating the current authoritative document over creating parallel versions.
- Use descriptive titles that include the event or project and, when helpful, the date or semester.
- Keep owners, deadlines, statuses, assumptions, and dependencies explicit.
- Use Spanish for Alpha's operational and public-facing documents unless the user requests another language.
- Keep terminology consistent within a document.
- Use tables for genuine structured data such as budgets, schedules, responsibilities, and attendee records; use prose or bullets for narrative context.

### Before delivery

- Re-check the live source for drift if the work is date-sensitive.
- Confirm names, dates, venue, cost, capacity, status, and approvals.
- Check alignment with the mission, strategic pillar, institutional rules, and brand.
- Check owner coverage and cross-area handoffs.
- Remove placeholders only when a verified answer exists.
- Proofread Spanish and ensure the deliverable is easy for the responsible team to execute.
- State clearly what remains tentative, blocked, or awaiting approval.

## 14. Default response style for agents

- Lead with the requested result or decision-ready summary.
- Be concise, concrete, and operational.
- Use the same language as the user unless there is a reason not to.
- Do not bury unresolved decisions inside polished prose.
- For plans, show owners, dates, dependencies, and next actions.
- For reviews, separate factual errors, operational risks, brand issues, and optional improvements.
- Do not overstate completion: a draft is not approved, an invitation is not a confirmation, and a planned event is not an executed event.
